# References and acknowledgements

Citations beside a definition or formula identify the result being used.
References to related computational work do not mean that the package
implements the algorithms in those sources.

## Definitions and mathematical results

- The definitions of the Davenport, Erdős–Ginzburg–Ziv and eta constants
  follow {cite:t}`gao-geroldinger-2006`, Definition 2.1. This survey uses
  both `d(G)` and `D(G)`, with `D(G) = d(G) + 1`.
- The exact Davenport formulas for abelian p-groups and groups of rank at
  most two are due to Kruyswijk and Olson; see
  {cite:t}`gao-geroldinger-2006`, Theorem 3.1, and the original Olson
  papers {cite:p}`olson-1969-i,olson-1969-ii`.
- The logarithmic upper bound is due to
  {cite:t}`van-emde-boas-kruyswijk-1969`, Theorem 7.1, p. 21.
  Their `mu(G)` is the package's `D(G)`. The result is also recorded in
  {cite:t}`gao-geroldinger-2006`, Theorem 3.6(1).
  {py:attr}`zero_sum_sequences.AdditiveSequenceSpace.davenport_bound`
  uses a conservative integer relaxation, not the original real-valued bound.
- {cite:t}`geroldinger-halter-koch-2006` provides the factorization-theoretic
  background, including atoms and length sets.

## Related computational work

- {cite:t}`zerdoum-2021`, Chapters IV–V, develops sequence-propagation
  methods with interval-compressed representations.
- {cite:t}`geroldinger-liebmann-philipp-2012`, Section 4, describes a
  sequence-extension search for long zero-sum free sequences. Its
  introduction also records the logarithmic Davenport estimate.

These are useful comparisons for the package's exhaustive avoidance
search, not references for the details of its symmetry reduction or
subsequence-sum representation. The relevant API docstrings describe the
search and its completeness assumptions.

## Software

The core library uses [NetworkX](https://networkx.org/). SageMath is an
optional integration, not a requirement. Tutorial visualization uses
Matplotlib; the tutorial is a marimo notebook, run in the browser with Pyodide.
The documentation is built with Sphinx and the Shibuya theme.

## Use of AI

Large language models from OpenAI and Anthropic were used extensively
in developing this library. The author generally drafted the architecture,
then reviewed and refined the design with AI assistance. Much of the
implementation was generated by these models and subsequently reviewed
and revised by the author.

The author is responsible for the final code, documentation, and
mathematical claims.
